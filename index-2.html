Structure

Duration: 30 mins
Client: Chinwendu + Chinwe
Format: Group video call
Goal: Verbal yes in principle
⚡ North Star for This Call

Get both founders to articulate their biggest pain in their own words — then show them you already have the answer. Leave with a verbal yes and a confirmed follow-up date.

Key intel going in

She already said "we don't even have a website" — she's half-aware. Don't over-explain what she already knows.

Unknown going in

Which business is primary — the lash service or the studio rental? Everything else follows from this.

Your team on the call

You lead discovery. Partner leads the offer delivery. Agree this split before you dial in.

Watch out for

Chinwe (co-founder) going quiet. If she doesn't speak in first 10 mins, address her directly.

0–5 min
Open — Set the Frame
Rapport
▶
What you're doing

Warm, brief. Establish credibility without a CV. Set the agenda so they know what to expect. This kills anxiety on both sides.

How to open (Michael leads)
Michael
"Thanks for making the time — really appreciate it. So the plan for today: we want to spend most of the call just listening and understanding where you are right now with the business. Then towards the end, we'll share what we're thinking in terms of how we can help. Sound good?"
Michael — brief credibility, 30 seconds max
"Quick context on us — I work in digital systems in the NHS, I've been building and managing digital infrastructure for a few years. My partner has a background in digital marketing and business growth. We're specifically working with small businesses to help them build proper digital foundations — and Lush Lash Atelier caught our attention."
💡
Don't say "we noticed your page is small" or anything that sounds like criticism. You're framing interest, not diagnosis — yet.
⚠️
If Chinwe is quiet at the start, address her directly: "Chinwe, great to have you on — what's your role day-to-day in the business?" Get her talking early.
5–18 min
Discovery — Find the Real Pain
Listen
▶
Your job here

You already know the answers from your research. These questions aren't about finding out — they're about getting them to say it in their own words. That's what makes the offer land later.

Round 1 — Understand the business (2–3 mins)
Q1
"Tell me a bit about Lush Lash Atelier — how did it start, and how has it evolved?"
Gets them talking. You'll hear whether lash service or studio rental feels like the main thing to them.
Q2
"Right now, where's the main revenue coming from — the lash services, the studio rentals, or both?"
This is your fork in the road question. Their answer dictates which offer you make.
Round 2 — The booking journey (3–4 mins)
Q3
"When someone finds you on TikTok or Instagram, what typically happens next — walk me through it from their side."
Forces them to describe the broken journey themselves. Don't tell them it's broken.
Q4
"How many enquiries would you say you get a week? And of those, how many actually book?"
Surfaces the conversion gap. If they don't know the number, that itself is the signal.
Q5
"What happens when you're not available to respond to a DM straight away?"
Gets them to admit leads are slipping. They already feel this — you're just surfacing it.
Round 3 — Ambition + pain (3–4 mins)
Q6
"Where do you want the business to be in 12 months — more lash clients, more studio renters, or something else?"
Shows you're thinking about their future, not just their present.
Q7
"If you had to name one thing that, if fixed, would make the biggest difference to the business right now — what would it be?"
Golden question. Whatever they say becomes the anchor for your offer.
⚠️
If Chinwe still hasn't spoken much, ask her directly at Q6 or Q7: "Chinwe, from your side — what's the gap you feel most?"
💡
You won't need all 7 questions. Read the room. If they give you the pain clearly at Q3, move forward. Don't over-ask.
🔀 Discovery pivot — based on Q2 answer
Scenario A — Lash service is primary
▶

They want more lash clients. The studio is secondary or just monetising downtime.

What to listen for: frustration around missed bookings, not being able to respond fast enough, clients not coming back, no way for new clients to find them.

Lean into Q3–Q5. The broken booking journey is the core pain.

Scenario B — Studio rental is primary
▶

They want more lash/brow artists renting the space. The business model is B2B, not B2C.

What to listen for: inconsistent rental income, artists not returning, difficulty being found by artists looking for space, no way to show credibility to artists they don't know.

Lean into Q6–Q7. The trust and discoverability gap is the core pain.

Scenario C — Both matter (most likely)
▶

They're running two businesses from one account and neither has a proper foundation.

Your move: Name it clearly. "You've actually got two audiences — your lash clients and the artists renting your space. They need different things from you online. Right now they're getting the same page, which means neither is getting what they need."

Then ask: "Which one do you want to fix first?" — force the decision before you pitch.

18–27 min
Directional Offer — The Pitch
Offer
▶
How to transition (Michael hands to partner)
Michael — transition line
"That's really helpful — thank you both. Based on what you've just told us, I want to hand over to [Partner name] who's going to share what we're thinking in terms of how we can help."
Universal opening — always use this first
"You've got two things working in your favour that a lot of businesses at your stage don't have — a clear brand identity and content that's already getting real traction. That 10k view on the space tour isn't luck, it's proof the interest is there. The problem is there's nowhere for that interest to land. Someone watches that video, goes to your profile, can't figure out how to book, and moves on. That's what we want to fix."
🎯 Offer — based on their priority
Offer A — Lash service focus
▶
"What we want to build for you is a simple, professional website — your services, your pricing, your work, and a way for people to book directly without needing to DM and wait. We link it to your Instagram and TikTok so every video drives people somewhere that actually converts. And we set up your Google Business profile properly so when someone in Abuja searches for lash services, you show up — with reviews."

Impact frame: "The Brow Hut in Abuja has 739 Google reviews and a live booking calendar. They're not doing better lashes than you — they just made it easier to find them and easier to say yes. That's the gap we're closing."

Offer B — Studio rental focus
▶
"What we want to build is a dedicated landing page for the studio — clear photos of the space, your pricing tiers, what's included, and a simple booking form. Linked directly from your TikTok bio and Instagram. Right now your 10k-view video sends people to a profile with 48 followers and a DM instruction. We want to send them somewhere that closes the deal for you while you're sleeping."

Impact frame: "Artists looking for a professional space in Abuja are Googling right now. If you're not findable with a credible presence, they book someone else — not because the other space is better, but because they could see it and trust it."

Offer C — Both businesses
▶
"You've got two audiences — lash clients and studio renters — and they need different things from you online. We want to build a website that serves both: a clean home for the brand, with a section for lash bookings and a section for artists looking to rent. One place that covers both. We start with whichever is the priority for you right now, and we build the second in phase two."

Impact frame: "Right now both audiences are landing on a social media page with 48 TikTok followers and a DM instruction. That's not a reflection of the quality of what you're offering — it's just a missing foundation. We build that foundation."

The free framing — say this clearly
Partner or Michael
"Just to be clear on how we work at this stage — we're offering this at no cost. We're building our portfolio of case studies and we want to work with businesses we believe in. In return, we'd ask for your honest feedback and a testimonial once we've delivered. That's the deal."
🚨
Don't over-explain the free part or make it sound like charity. Say it once, clearly, then move on. If they push on "why free" — "We're selective about who we work with. We saw potential here."
27–30 min
Close — Next Steps
Close
▶
Three things you need before the call ends
  • Verbal yes in principle from both of them.
    "Does this sound like the right direction for you both?" — wait for both to respond. Don't accept one nodding. Look at Chinwe directly.
  • Confirmed follow-up date
    "I'll send you a written outline of exactly what we'll deliver and when. Does Tuesday or Wednesday work for you to receive that?"
  • Confirmed point of contact
    "Chinwendu — are you the right person to send the follow-up to, or should I copy Chinwe in too?"
Closing line
Michael — closing
"Perfect. Really enjoyed this conversation — you've built something with real potential and we're looking forward to helping you take it further. Expect something from us by [day]. Thanks both."
🚨
Do not leave without a specific day committed. "Early next week" is not a next step. "Tuesday" is.
⚠️
If they raise objections ("we need to think about it", "we're quite busy") — don't push. Say: "Completely understand. What I'll do is send you the written outline anyway — then you've got something concrete to look at. Does that work?" Gets you the follow-up without pressure.
